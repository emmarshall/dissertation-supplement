Verdicts, Mental State and Legal Accuracy

October 10, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

This chapter reports the analyses of the narratives by verdict condition (RQ2, H2a, H2b, and H2c) and three exploratory analyses. These are the accuracy of participants’ legal judgments, the three-way interaction of abortion attitudes, state law, and judgment type, and the comparison of the narratives with television plotlines and documented cases.

Narrative Features by Verdict Condition (RQ2)

A series of descriptive chi-square tests compared the frequency of each feature across the four verdict conditions, with the Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied across the testable features (RQ2; see Table P1.10). After the correction, 12 features varied with the verdict participants were instructed to write toward, including legal terminology (\(\chi^2(3, N = 311) = 28.98\), BH-adjusted p < .001) and causal structure (V = .29).

Coded Factors by Verdict Condition (H2a and H2b)

H2a predicted that narratives written toward a wrongful verdict would contain more coded mitigating and aggravating factors than narratives written toward a rightful verdict. There was no significant difference in total coded factors per narrative across the four verdict conditions, F(3, 76) = 0.88, MSe = 0.33, p = .456, \(\eta^2\) = .034. Contrary to H2a, narratives in the wrongful conditions (M = 1.00, SD = 0.56) did not contain significantly more coded factors than narratives in the rightful conditions (M = 0.80, SD = 0.57; see Figure P1.2).

H2b predicted that the wrongful–rightful difference would be larger for mitigating than for aggravating factors. There was no significant interaction between verdict condition and factor type, F(3, 76) = 0.59, MSe = 0.13, p = .622, \(\eta_p^2\) = .023. Contrary to H2b, the wrongful–rightful difference was not larger for mitigating factors. However, there was a significant main effect of factor type: narratives contained more mitigating factors (M = 0.50, SD = 0.37) than aggravating factors (M = 0.39, SD = 0.39; F(1, 76) = 4.54, MSe = 0.13, p = .036, \(\eta_p^2\) = .056).

Mental State by Actual Culpability (H2c)

H2c rests on the coded mental-state category, so I report it as exploratory. H2c predicted that narratives in the culpable conditions would attribute purposeful or knowing mental states more often than narratives in the non-culpable conditions. The relationship between actual culpability and coded mental state was not significant, \(\chi^2\)(1, N = 279) = 0.32, p = .572. Contrary to H2c, coded mental state did not differ by actual culpability. However, the mental-state code did not meet the preregistered reliability target (ICC = .67, registered \(\kappa\) = .43, target = .80; see Table P1.3), leaving this result inconclusive. The code was not reliable enough to tell whether participants matched the mental state to the verdict they wrote toward or to the facts of the case.

Legal Accuracy of the Crime Judgments

State-law coding. For the legal-accuracy sub-analysis, participants’ descriptive judgments (“Is this a crime where you live?”) were compared with the legal status of each scenario in their state of residence as of May 2026. Participant responses were matched based on accuracy (yes/no). However, given that the specifics of state laws regarding abortion remain in flux and convoluted, I did not interpret a mismatch as true ignorance of the underlying state-specific status. For example, many state abortion bans do not apply to pregnant people as a matter of black letter law, but they still can be, and have been, used to criminalize abortion (Weigel et al., 2020), so whether self-managed abortion is a crime in a given state is not a clear binary. Appendix A of the dissertation gives the coding rules and sources for each state and the confidence flags used in the preregistered sensitivity analysis.

I report the legal-accuracy analysis as exploratory. Compared with the state-law coding, accuracy differed significantly from chance on 19 of the 20 scenarios. Accuracy on the nine state-invariant scenarios ranged from .95 to 1.00 (M = .98) and was lower on the six abortion scenarios with between-state variance (.64 to .82, M = .76). The per-scenario accuracy tallies appear in Table P1.9.

Abortion Attitudes, State Law, and Judgment Type

I report this analysis as exploratory. When the state-law coding was added to the mixed-effects model of card-sort crime judgments, the three-way Attitudes × State Law × Judgment Type interaction was significant (OR = 0.28, 95% CI [0.13, 0.63], z = −3.09, p = .002; see Table P1.7). Because Pilot 1 was not powered to detect three-way interactions (N = 81), I report this estimate descriptively.

Narratives Compared With Television Plotlines and Documented Cases

I report these comparisons as exploratory and descriptive. None was tested inferentially. As registered, I compared the abortion methods named in the narratives (i.e., the Section F method code) with the methods shown in U.S. television abortion plotlines (Herold & Sisson, 2019). I mapped the television categories onto the method code (see Table P1.11). The largest difference was for physical or instrument methods. They were named in .42 of the narratives that specified a method and in .67 of the procedures in the television plotlines.

The Section J code identified narratives referring to real-world cases. None of the 311 narratives referred to a documented real-world case. For the prosecution comparison, I used documented U.S. pregnancy-criminalization cases (Pregnancy Justice, 2023) in place of the registered television reference, which contains no account of actual prosecutions. I recorded this source change as a deviation. I prompted approximately .25 of the narratives for each crime category; these shares reflect the design rather than participants’ expectations. In documented prosecutions, charges for chemical endangerment (i.e., criminal child neglect, abuse, or endangerment) made up .84 of charged cases. Fetal homicide made up .06, improper disposal .03, and self-managed abortion .01. The balanced design therefore over-samples the three categories that are rare in actual prosecutions.
