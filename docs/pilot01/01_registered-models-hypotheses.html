Registered Analytic Models and Hypotheses

November 18, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

Analytic Models.

I conducted all analyses in R (R Core Team, 2026). Each research question was tested with its own model. Chi-square tests of independence compared the content of the narratives across the four crime categories (RQ1) and across the four verdict conditions (RQ2). These tests covered the 67 automated features (i.e., the Phase 1 and Phase 2 codes), with the Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied across that family. The 14 holistic codes (i.e., the Phase 3 codes) are complete. However, only one provided interpretable evidence that the preregistered reliability target had been met. I therefore used the holistic codes only in the analyses of mitigating and aggravating factors and mental states (H2a, H2b, H2c, and H3a), and I treat every result that rests on a holistic code as exploratory. I report Cramér’s V with a 95% confidence interval for each variable.

A within-subjects one-way ANOVA tested whether post-narrative severity ratings differed across the four crime categories (RQ3, H3b). Each participant gave four ratings, one for each crime category. I applied the Greenhouse–Geisser correction when sphericity was violated. I fit the ANOVA with the afex package, computed estimated marginal means and planned contrasts with emmeans (Lenth & Piaskowski, 2017), and computed effect sizes with the effectsize package. Registered planned contrasts and main effects were tested at α = .05 without adjustment. The one exception was the follow-up of interactions. I corrected the simple effects that probe the interaction of verdict condition and factor type (H2b) within that family. I used Tukey’s HSD when the follow-up compared all pairs of estimated marginal means and the Bonferroni correction when it tested a specified subset.

Two multiple regressions tested whether scores on the Modified Abortion Attitudes Scale predicted each participant’s mean severity and mean punishment ratings across the four crime categories (RQ4, H4). Age, political orientation, and religiosity were covariates. I compared the card-sort consensus with the preregistered threshold descriptively (RQ5). Mixed-effects logistic regressions tested whether the features of each scenario predicted the binary judgment that the scenario was “definitely yes, this is a crime” (H5). Each model had a random intercept for participants. I fit these models with the lme4 and lmerTest packages. A pooled McNemar’s test across the 20 scenarios compared descriptive and normative crime judgments (RQ6, H6). The legal-accuracy analysis used one-sample binomial tests against chance (.50), for each scenario and pooled across scenarios. I computed the McNemar and exact binomial tests with the exact2x2 package. Two mixed-effects logistic regressions of the card-sort crime judgments tested the attitude hypotheses (RQ7, H7a and H7b). The first model pooled the two judgment types. The second added the interaction of abortion attitudes and judgment type. Both models had crossed random intercepts for participants and scenarios, and both were fit with lme4 and lmerTest.

I report effect sizes (i.e., partial \(\eta^2\), Cramér’s V, and odds ratios) and 95% confidence intervals for all tests. The cluster analysis is exploratory. It used partitioning around medoids on Gower distance within each crime category. I fit it with the cluster package and evaluated it with the silhouette width and the gap statistic. I computed scale descriptives and reliability coefficients with the psych package. The text analyses of the narratives used quanteda (Benoit et al., 2018), with quanteda.textstats and quanteda.textplots for keyness and text statistics and udpipe for linguistic annotation. I computed inter-rater reliability (i.e., Cohen’s and Fleiss’s kappa) with the irr package.

The two kinds of mixed-effects logistic regression take the following multilevel form. In the feature-prediction models (H5), crime judgments \(i\) are nested within participants \(j\). Each scenario feature \(F\) enters as a single Level-1 predictor:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Level 1:} \quad & \mathrm{logit}\big(\mathrm{Pr}(\text{crime}_{ij} = 1)\big) = \beta_{0j} + \beta_{1}\,F_{ij} \\ \text{Level 2:} \quad & \beta_{0j} = \gamma_{00} + u_{0j}, \quad u_{0j} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\ \tau^{2}_{\text{participant}}). \end{aligned} \]

The attitude models (H7a and H7b) cross each judgment with both participants \(j\) and scenarios \(k\). Standardized abortion attitudes (\(\text{AAS}_j\)) enter as a participant-level (Level-2) predictor, and judgment type (descriptive vs. normative) enters as a Level-1 predictor:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Level 1:} \quad & \mathrm{logit}\big(\mathrm{Pr}(\text{crime}_{ijk} = 1)\big) = \beta_{0jk} + \beta_{1jk}\,\text{JudgmentType}_{ijk} \\ \text{Level 2:} \quad & \beta_{0jk} = \gamma_{00} + \gamma_{01}\,\text{AAS}_{j} + u_{0j} + v_{0k} \\ & \beta_{1jk} = \gamma_{10} + \gamma_{11}\,\text{AAS}_{j} \\ & u_{0j} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\ \tau^{2}_{\text{participant}}), \quad v_{0k} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\ \tau^{2}_{\text{scenario}}). \end{aligned} \]

H7b tests whether the association between abortion attitudes and crime judgments differs by judgment type (\(\gamma_{11}\)). H7a tests the main-effects model, in which \(\gamma_{11} = 0\).

Hypotheses.

I grouped the seven preregistered Pilot 1 questions by the judgments participants made. RQ1 to RQ3 concerned the narratives: whether their features, mitigating and aggravating factors, and attributed mental states differed across crime categories and verdict conditions. RQ4 and RQ7 concerned abortion attitudes, measured with the Modified Abortion Attitudes Scale, as predictors of severity, punishment, and card-sort crime judgments. I predicted a stronger association with normative than descriptive judgments. The card-sort analyses (RQ5 and RQ6) tested whether at least four scenarios met the preregistered criterion for broad disagreement, whether the features of the scenarios predicted the crime judgments, and whether descriptive and normative judgments diverged. The full hypothesis statements and analysis plan are in the preregistration on the study’s OSF page.1
