Pilot Study 2 Materials and Measures

November 18, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

The stimulus variations and study-specific survey questions are reproduced below. The stimulus materials and registered Pilot Study 2 materials and measures are also available.

Stimulus Wording

A. Attention Checks

  1. To show you are reading each question carefully, please choose ‘Neither Agree nor Disagree.’
  2. We want to make sure your responses are thoughtful. Please choose ‘Strongly Agree’ for this item.
  3. Please read all instructions carefully. For this question, select ‘Disagree’ as your answer.

B. Subjective Probability of Guilt

Probability (0–100%) · 0–100 slider · Single item.

Item text (identical at both timings): “Based on the evidence available at the time the warrant was sought, what is the probability that the suspect committed the crime the detective suspects her of committing?”

Response: 0–100 slider, labeled Probability (0–100%); the selected value displays with a percent sign.

Post-search debias preamble (precedes the item at the post-search timing): “Now that you have seen the results of the search, please re-rate the affidavit using the same scale, but think back to the time when the detective decided to seek the warrant and answer as if you did not know what the search ultimately revealed.”

C.0. Gestational-Age Estimate (Post-Search, Block End; Once per Search Scenario) (Crawford et al., 2022)

Item (single choice, request response): “Based only on what you read, about how far along do you think the pregnancy was when the loss occurred?”

  1. Early in the pregnancy (first trimester, roughly 1–12 weeks)
  2. Around the middle (early second trimester, roughly 13–20 weeks)
  3. Later in the pregnancy (late second trimester or beyond, roughly 21+ weeks)
  4. Unsure / can’t tell from the affidavit

C. Outcome Likelihood Estimates (Evelo & Greene, 2013; Fischhoff, 1975)

0% — 100% per outcome · Four items, fixed order, rated independently; ratings need not sum to 100%.

Pre-search instructions (foresight framing): “You are the judge who has been asked to authorize this search. Based only on the affidavit you just read and what the detective knew when seeking the warrant, please rate the likelihood of each of the following outcomes of the requested search by writing a probability value from 0% to 100% next to each outcome below. There are many possible outcomes; please rate each outcome independently.”

Post-search instructions (hindsight framing with debias instruction): “Think back to the time when the detective asked you to authorize the search, before it was carried out. Putting yourself back at that moment and deciding based only on what was known then, as if you did not know what the search ultimately revealed, please rate the likelihood of each of the following outcomes of the requested search by writing a probability value from 0% to 100% next to each outcome below. There are many possible outcomes; please rate each outcome independently.”

Four items, fixed order in every condition:

  1. The search reveals evidence that the suspect illegally obtained an abortion to end her own pregnancy. ____%
  2. The search reveals evidence that the suspect improperly disposed of the fetal remains or concealed the loss following a miscarriage or stillbirth. ____%
  3. The search reveals evidence that the suspect committed a financial crime, such as stolen credit cards and associated records. ____%
  4. The search reveals nothing illegal or incriminating. ____%

D. Foreseeability Impressions Items Index (Blank et al., 2008)

Strongly disagree Strongly agree
1 2 3 4 5

Item 2 reverse-scored; four items averaged into the composite, plus a single general-foreseeability item.

Instructions (both timings): “Please indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with each of the following statements as a description of the scenario you read about.”

Pre-search (future tense):

  1. The detective knows, at the time he seeks the warrant, what the search will reveal.
  2. It is difficult for the detective to predict what the search will reveal when he decides to seek the warrant. (reverse-scored)
  3. The detective has a clear and vivid idea of what the search will reveal at the time he seeks the warrant.
  4. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, the outcome of the search is foreseeable.
  5. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, it is foreseeable that the search will uncover evidence of the offense described in the affidavit.

Post-search (past tense):

  1. The detective would have known all along what the search would reveal at the time he sought the warrant.
  2. It was difficult for the detective to predict what the search would reveal when he decided to seek the warrant. (reverse-scored)
  3. The detective had a clear and vivid idea of what the search would reveal at the time he sought the warrant.
  4. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, the outcome of the search was foreseeable.
  5. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, it was foreseeable that the search would uncover evidence of the offense described in the affidavit.

E. Quality of the Detective’s Decision (Blank et al., 2015)

Strongly disagree Strongly agree
1 2 3 4 5 6 7

Five items averaged into a composite.

The post-search instruction adds a re-rating lead-in to the pre-search instruction; the five items are tensed by timing, present tense pre-search (the foresight block) and past tense post-search, parallel to the foreseeability scale.

Instructions (pre-search): “Think back to the time when the detective decided to seek the warrant and rate the quality of his decision.”

Instructions (post-search): “Now that you have seen the results of the search, please rate the quality of the detective’s decision again. Think back to the time when the detective decided to seek the warrant and rate the quality of his decision.”

Pre-search (present tense):

  1. The process leading to the detective’s decision to seek the warrant is clear and transparent.
  2. The detective’s decision is fair and unbiased.
  3. Any reasonable detective in this position would make the same decision.
  4. The decision is made responsibly, taking all relevant information into account.
  5. Overall, it is a good decision to seek the warrant.

Post-search (past tense):

  1. The process leading to the detective’s decision to seek the warrant was clear and transparent.
  2. The detective’s decision was fair and unbiased.
  3. Any reasonable detective in this position would have made the same decision.
  4. The decision was made responsibly, taking all relevant information into account.
  5. Overall, it was a good decision to seek the warrant.

F. Severity of Consequences

Not at all serious Extremely serious
1 2 3 4 5 6 7

Single item.

Item text (both timings): “How serious are the consequences of what happened in this case?”

Response: 7-point scale (1 = not at all serious to 7 = extremely serious).

F.2. Seriousness of the Revealed Crime (Post-Search Only) (Sood, 2015)

Not at all serious Extremely serious
1 2 3 4 5 6 7

Single item.

Item text (post-search only): “How serious is the crime that the search uncovered?”

Response: 7-point scale (1 = not at all serious to 7 = extremely serious).

G. Ratings of the Search Scenario

Two 9-point subscales (believability; relevance) · 10 narrative evidence items, the same list at every administration · Each subscale is administered as a 10-statement matrix with drop-down responses, the instruction as the matrix stem on the same page, and statement order randomized per participant.

The relevance dimension is adapted from the Casper et al. (1989) and Robbennolt & Sobus (1997) evidence-reinterpretation paradigm. The believability dimension is taken verbatim from Marshall (2022).

G.a Believability Ratings

Instructions (matrix stem, pre-search only): “Please rate how believable you think it is that each of the following items from the situation leading up to the search would actually occur in a real life situation:”

Response: 9-point scale (1 = not at all believable to 9 = extremely believable), one rating per evidence item.

G.b Relevance Ratings (Casper et al., 1989)

Pre-search instructions (future tense, foresight variant; matrix stem): “Next, we would like you to rate how relevant you think each of the following items from the situation leading up to the search would be to what would actually be found as a result of the search of the suspect’s phone:”

Post-search instructions (past tense, hindsight variant; matrix stem): “Next, we would like you to rate how relevant you think each of the following items from the situation leading up to the search were to what was actually found as a result of the search of the suspect’s phone:”

Response: 9-point scale (1 = not at all relevant to 9 = extremely relevant), one rating per evidence item.

Evidence Items (per Search Scenario)

SMA vignette (Mary Hansen)

  1. That an anonymous coworker observed that the suspect had been pregnant and was no longer pregnant.
  2. That the suspect appeared to be dating around and did not have a long-term romantic partner.
  3. That the suspect was enrolled as a community college student.
  4. That the suspect had not told her parents, workplace, or friends about her pregnancy.
  5. That the suspect had sought reproductive care through a telehealth service rather than a local hospital or clinic.
  6. That the suspect said she was alone when she lost her pregnancy at home.
  7. That the suspect put the remains in a bag and buried it outside Millfield.
  8. That the suspect went to a hair appointment soon after losing her pregnancy.
  9. That the suspect experienced cramping and heavy bleeding but did not seek medical care immediately.
  10. That the suspect was 19 years old and lived with a roommate.

NCM vignette (Mary Johnston)

  1. That an anonymous coworker observed that the suspect was no longer pregnant.
  2. That the suspect had been trying to conceive and was openly excited about her pregnancy.
  3. That the suspect had held a stable job for several years.
  4. That the suspect had told her husband, her doctor, and her workplace about her pregnancy.
  5. That the suspect had been receiving prenatal care from an obstetrician.
  6. That the suspect said she was alone when she lost her pregnancy at home.
  7. That the suspect retrieved the remains from the toilet and buried them in her backyard.
  8. That the suspect took time off work to recover after losing her pregnancy.
  9. That the suspect experienced cramping and heavy bleeding and went to the hospital, where she was treated for hemorrhage and retained tissue.
  10. That the suspect was 32 years old and lived with her husband.

CM vignette (Mary Larson)

  1. That an anonymous coworker reported the suspect had hidden her pregnancy and lost it at home.
  2. That the suspect had been in an on-and-off relationship with her boyfriend and was not married.
  3. That the suspect had recently started a retail job.
  4. That the suspect had not told her family, her roommate, or her boyfriend about her pregnancy.
  5. That the suspect had taken only a home pregnancy test and had not seen any medical provider during her pregnancy.
  6. That the suspect said she was alone when she lost her pregnancy at home.
  7. That the suspect tried to flush the remains, and when the toilet backed up, put them in a bag in the trash.
  8. That the suspect kept a hair appointment and worked her shift soon after losing her pregnancy.
  9. That the suspect experienced cramping and heavy bleeding but did not seek medical care immediately.
  10. That the suspect was 25 years old and lived with a roommate.

H. Suspect Mens Rea (Model Penal Code Culpability Hierarchy; Post-Search Only) (Kneer & Skoczeń, 2023)

Strongly disagree Strongly agree
1 2 3 4 5 6 7

Four items, rated separately (no composite).

Instructions (post-search only): “Please indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with each of the following statements about the suspect’s state of mind.”

  1. It was the suspect’s purposeful objective to end the pregnancy. (purposefully)
  2. The suspect was practically certain that the pregnancy would end. (knowingly)
  3. The suspect was aware of a substantial risk that the pregnancy would end. (recklessly)
  4. The suspect should have been aware of a substantial risk that the pregnancy would end. (negligently)

I. Suspect’s Moral Culpability (Post-Search Only) (Alicke, 2000)

Three single-item 7-point measures, rated separately (no composite); anchors as shown per item.

Blame item (1 = not at all blameworthy to 7 = extremely blameworthy): “To what extent is the suspect blameworthy for what happened?”

Responsibility item (1 = not at all responsible to 7 = completely responsible): “How responsible is the suspect for what happened?”

Punishment item (1 = no punishment at all to 7 = very severe punishment): “How much punishment does the suspect deserve for what happened?”

Multi-Model Blame Items (Alicke et al., 2008; Cushman et al., 2008; Malle et al., 2014)

Five single-item 7-point measures, rated separately; anchors as shown per item.

Causality item (1 = not at all a cause to 7 = very strong cause): “To what extent did the suspect’s actions cause the pregnancy loss?” Verbatim from Alicke et al. (2008) (Study 1), anchor adapted from 11-point to 7-point.

Reasons item (1 = not at all justified to 7 = completely justified): “Did the suspect have justified reasons for her conduct during the pregnancy?” We wrote this item, grounded in the Guglielmo & Malle (2017) Table 1 reasons template.

Preventability item (1 = definitely not to 7 = definitely yes): “Could the suspect have prevented the pregnancy loss?” Single combined unintentional-branch item. We wrote this item, grounded in the Guglielmo & Malle (2017) Table 1 preventability template.

Character item (1 = very unfavorable to 7 = very favorable): “Given what you know about the suspect from these case materials, do you have a generally favorable or unfavorable impression of her?” Verbatim from Alicke et al. (2008) (Study 3), anchor adapted from 11-point to 7-point.

Wrongness item (1 = not at all to 4 = somewhat to 7 = very much): “Was the suspect’s conduct during the pregnancy morally wrong?” Verbatim from Cushman et al. (2008) (Experiment 1).

J. Detective’s Moral Culpability (Post-Search Only)

Three items · 0–10 scales · anchors as shown per item.

  1. To what extent is the detective blameworthy for his decision to seek the warrant? (0 = not at all blameworthy; 10 = extremely blameworthy)
  2. How severe a consequence should the detective face for his decision to seek the warrant? (0 = no consequence at all; 10 = very severe consequence)
  3. How right or wrong was the detective’s decision to seek the warrant? (0 = not wrong at all; 10 = very wrong)

K. Search-Target Endorsement (Pre-Search, Administered Once)

Target 1: Reverse keyword warrant (lowest invasiveness). “A court order directing a technology company to identify any person who recently searched for specified pregnancy-related or abortion-related terms on the company’s search engine.” (Kerr, 2025)

Target 2: Provider text-message content warrant (moderate invasiveness). “A warrant served on the suspect’s cellular-service provider compelling disclosure of the content of the suspect’s text messages.” (Stored Communications Act § 2703(d)) (Conti-Cook & Bertash, 2021; Dellinger & Pell, 2024)

Target 3: Physical home search (highest invasiveness). “A warrant authorizing law enforcement officers to physically search the suspect’s home.” (Baucum et al., 2018)

  1. Binary endorsement. “Would you approve of a law enforcement officer conducting this search?” (Yes / No)
  2. Certainty threshold. “What percentage certain would you have to be that the suspect committed the offense described in the affidavit before approving this search?” (0–100 slider, labeled Certainty required (0–100%))
  3. Probability of evidence given guilt. “If the suspect had indeed committed the offense described in the affidavit, what is the probability that this search would uncover evidence of it?” (0–100 slider, labeled Probability (0–100%)) (Baucum et al., 2018).

L. Manipulation Checks

L.a Per-Scenario Comprehension Check

Prompt (in every vignette block): “Please indicate which of the following statements are true about the case you just read. Check all that apply.”

SMA vignette (Mary Hansen)

  1. The suspect identified in the affidavit was named Mary Hansen.
  2. The suspect told the detective she had visited a single telemedicine appointment with an unnamed provider.
  3. The suspect called emergency services immediately after the pregnancy loss.

NCM vignette (Mary Johnston)

  1. The suspect identified in the affidavit was named Mary Johnston.
  2. The suspect was married and had been actively trying to conceive.
  3. The suspect did not disclose the pregnancy to her workplace.

CM vignette (Mary Larson)

  1. The suspect identified in the affidavit was named Mary Larson.
  2. The suspect tried to flush the remains but the toilet backed up, then placed the remains in a bag and put it in the trash.
  3. The suspect disclosed the pregnancy to her boyfriend.

In every block, items 1 and 2 are true and item 3 is false; the three were presented in random order.

L.b Manipulation Check

Item: “What did the search reveal at the end of each affidavit you read?”

  • Evidence consistent with the suspect’s intentional termination of the pregnancy, concealment of the loss, or improper disposal of remains (correct in the HACE condition)
  • Evidence of unrelated credit-card fraud (correct in the HUCE condition)
  • I cannot recall what the search revealed

Options were presented in random order. A participant passed the check by selecting the option that matched their assigned outcome.

M–R. Individual-Difference Measures

Pilot 2, Study 1, and Study 2 administered the same six individual-difference scales (the Modified Abortion Attitude Scale, Support for Punishment of Women for Miscarriage, the Ambivalent Sexism Inventory short form, the religiosity scale, the political-orientation items, and the Outcome Preference Measure), provided in the registered Pilot Study 2 materials and measures.

S. Demographics

The demographic items administered in Pilot 2 are provided in the registered Pilot Study 2 materials and measures.
