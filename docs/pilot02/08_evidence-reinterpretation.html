Evidence Reinterpretation (RQ3)

October 10, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

RH1.4 was supported on the respecified item-level model (Table P2.23). The interaction of timing, outcome, and key consistency was 0.77, 95% HDI [0.52, 1.02], Pr(effect > 0) > .999, BF₁₀ > 1000. The 95% HDI meets the registered rule that it lie above zero. The model met the registered convergence criteria.

I respecified the model because the registered model nests item consistency within outcome. The respecified model codes each item, for every participant, by whether it would be consistent with the expected outcome. After inspecting the raw means, I chose to compare the same items across the randomly assigned outcomes. I made that choice before fitting the revised model. That timing makes the test less severe (see item 9.1 of the Pilot 2 deviations record on the study’s OSF page).

The registered model tests the interaction of timing and item consistency, with NCM as the reference vignette (Table P2.24). Its 95% HDI meets the registered criterion of a positive interval. Its registered convergence criteria were met (worst within-imputation R-hat = 1.002, lowest effective sample size = 2250, total divergent transitions = 0). In the eight-item sensitivity analysis without items 3 and 10, the result of the registered model holds (0.68 [0.49, 0.87]). The result of the respecified model does not hold (0.23 [−0.08, 0.54]). Without those two items, its key contrast rests on the NCM vignette alone (see item 9.2 of the deviations record). The change in the observed composite is descriptive. The exploratory tests for each item below keep the registered grouping by vignette.

The registered model had a divergent transition. To address it, I refit the model on the fourth imputed data set with adapt_delta = .99 (previously .95) and kept the other four fits. The model, priors, data, random seeds, initial values, and iteration counts did not change.
