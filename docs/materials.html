Study Materials

November 18, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

This page lists the full materials for each study: the Pilot Study 1 narrative codebook and, for Pilot Study 2, Study 1 and Study 2, the vignette page participants read in each condition, with the document images that page shows, in order. Each vignette page is the participant-facing page embedded in the survey; it also holds the instructions and the text passages participants read between the images. The images here are reduced copies; the vignette pages show them at full size.

Measures Across Studies

Selected stimulus images, full materials, and item wording are linked in the Pilot Study 2 materials, Study 1 materials, and Study 2 materials. The shared individual-difference scales and demographic items are in the dissertation’s Individual-Difference Scales and Demographic Items section.

Table 2 lists the measures administered in each study and the changes in wording, response scale, or compositing rules between studies. All three studies shared a core of scenario-dependent judgment measures and individual-difference scales; Study 2 added several measures that the earlier studies did not include.

Note. Yes = administered; — = not administered. Three structural differences run through the table. First, Pilot 2 and Study 1 used a search-warrant paradigm, in which the detective sought a warrant and participants evaluated the search, whereas Study 2 used an arrest paradigm, in which the detective arrested the suspect and participants served as jurors evaluating probable cause and damages; the referent wording changed accordingly. Second, Pilot 2 and Study 1 referred to “the suspect,” whereas Study 2 named the suspect (Ms. Johnston) and the detective (Detective Snyder). Third, Study 1 tensed its items by cell (present or future tense in foresight, past tense in hindsight), whereas Study 2 used the past tense throughout, with a conditional framing (“as if you did not know”) in the no-outcome condition. Individual-difference scale items and attention checks were identical across the three studies.

a The seriousness-of-the-revealed-crime item was administered only after the search outcome information, once participants had seen what the search revealed.
