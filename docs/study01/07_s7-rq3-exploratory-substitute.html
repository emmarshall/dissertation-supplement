Notes shared by tables of this section.

  • Tables 1-S7.1 to 1-S7.3: The judgments tested here are rated on the 1–7 scale; differences and means are in scale points.
  • Tables 1-S7.1 to 1-S7.17: Expected evidence = evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion; unexpected evidence = financial-crime evidence.
  • Tables 1-S7.1 to 1-S7.3 and 1-S7.5 to 1-S7.18: n per condition, primary sample: Illegal abortion: No information 68, Expected evidence 63, Unexpected evidence 63; Improper disposal: No information 71, Expected evidence 66, Unexpected evidence 67 (all 398 in the analysis).
  • Tables 1-S7.2 and 1-S7.4: n per condition, sensitivity sample: Illegal abortion: No information 55, Expected evidence 56, Unexpected evidence 54; Improper disposal: No information 56, Expected evidence 62, Unexpected evidence 58 (all 341 in the analysis).
  • Tables 1-S7.4 and 1-S7.7 to 1-S7.14: Blame, punishment, the four mental states, responsibility and wrongness are rated on the 1–7 scale; differences are in scale points.
  • Tables 1-S7.5, 1-S7.7, 1-S7.8, 1-S7.14 and 1-S7.15: Intervals: two-sided 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (type 6) from participant resamples stratified within the six conditions, 10,000 attempted and 10,000 usable.
  • Tables 1-S7.5 and 1-S7.15: The model is just-identified, so it has no global fit.
  • Tables 1-S7.5, 1-S7.7, 1-S7.14 and 1-S7.15: A letter is supported when its interval lies on the predicted side of zero.
  • Tables 1-S7.5 and 1-S7.6: Every row of this analysis uses pregnancy-evidence likelihood as the substitute mediator (deviation 2 in S1).
  • Tables 1-S7.6 and 1-S7.17: Unstandardized estimates in the original units with robust (Huber–White) standard errors, normal Wald 95% intervals and two-sided p.
  • Tables 1-S7.6 and 1-S7.17: β = the standardized estimate (std.all) of the same path; inference and decisions use the unstandardized estimates.
  • Tables 1-S7.7 and 1-S7.8: Differences of adjusted outcome-condition means, each the equal-weight average of the two within-context differences, from analyses of covariance with the four attitudes as covariates.
  • Tables 1-S7.7, 1-S7.8 and 1-S7.14: No item was missing, so no values were imputed.
  • Tables 1-S7.9, 1-S7.11 and 1-S7.12: Interval: Normal Wald, two-sided 95%; the test statistic and p from the same standard error. n = 398.
  • Tables 1-S7.9, 1-S7.11 and 1-S7.12: I calculated the comparisons jointly from the model of all four ratings.
  • Tables 1-S7.9, 1-S7.11 and 1-S7.12: The model has an outcome-condition intercept in each legal context and unrestricted residual covariances.
  • Tables 1-S7.9, 1-S7.11 and 1-S7.12: The comparisons hold attitudes at their means and include variation in that attitude reference in their standard errors.
