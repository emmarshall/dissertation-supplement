Notes shared by tables of this section.

  • Tables 1-S9.1, 1-S9.2 and 1-S9.4 to 1-S9.6: No model variable was missing, so no values were imputed.
  • Tables 1-S9.1 and 1-S9.4: Predicted = the registered sign; rows without a letter have no registered direction and are reported without a verdict.
  • Tables 1-S9.1, 1-S9.4 and 1-S9.5: A letter is supported when its interval lies on the predicted side of zero.
  • Tables 1-S9.1 and 1-S9.4: Expected evidence = evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion; unexpected evidence = financial-crime evidence.
  • Tables 1-S9.1 and 1-S9.4: n per condition, primary sample: Illegal abortion: No information 68, Expected evidence 63, Unexpected evidence 63; Improper disposal: No information 71, Expected evidence 66, Unexpected evidence 67 (all 398 in the analysis).

Figure 1-S9.1

Adjusted mean certainty required for each search target, overall and by outcome information

Note. Adjusted mean certainty required for each search target from the joint model of the three certainty ratings, with attitudes at their primary-sample means. The left panel compares the targets overall, with adjacent differences corresponding to H5a and H5b. In the right panel, the two legal contexts have equal weight within each outcome condition. Error bars represent two-sided 95% t confidence intervals for the means, based on the model’s residual degrees of freedom. The interval calculation combines the joint model covariance and the sampling covariance of the attitude means, as in the registered comparisons. Expected evidence is evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion. Unexpected evidence is evidence of a financial crime. Primary sample n per condition: illegal abortion, 68 with no information, 63 with expected evidence, and 63 with unexpected evidence; improper disposal, 71 with no information, 66 with expected evidence, and 67 with unexpected evidence. All 398 participants are in the analysis.
