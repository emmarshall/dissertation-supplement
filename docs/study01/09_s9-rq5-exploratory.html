Figure S9.1

Adjusted mean certainty required for each search target, overall and by outcome information

Note. Adjusted mean certainty required for each search target from the joint model of the three certainty ratings, with attitudes at their primary-sample means. The left panel compares the targets overall, with adjacent differences corresponding to H5a and H5b. In the right panel, the two legal contexts have equal weight within each outcome condition. Error bars represent two-sided 95% t confidence intervals for the means, based on the model’s residual degrees of freedom. The interval calculation combines the joint model covariance and the sampling covariance of the attitude means, as in the registered comparisons. Expected evidence is evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion. Unexpected evidence is evidence of a financial crime. Primary sample n per condition: illegal abortion, 68 with no information, 63 with expected evidence, and 63 with unexpected evidence; improper disposal, 71 with no information, 66 with expected evidence, and 67 with unexpected evidence. All 398 participants are in the analysis.

Table S9.6

Target-matched likelihood rating at which approval of each search target becomes more likely than not (E15g)

Target Threshold 95% CI Curve Resamples Rank Decision
Note. Threshold: the likelihood rating (0–100 scale) at which the average predicted probability of approval of the target in the saved E15f model is one half, the six conditions weighted equally and the attitudes at their pooled means; none when the curve does not cross one half inside the scale. Rank orders the thresholds found, lowest first. Intervals: two-sided 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (type 6) from participant resamples, 10,000 attempted, each interval computed only from the resamples in which the curve crossed one half. Resamples: the number of usable resamples in which the curve crossed one half, of those attempted; no interval is given without a threshold or with fewer than two crossings. No model variable was missing, so no values were imputed. Reported without a verdict (no registered decision rule; the registration explores whether the thresholds rise from reverse keyword warrants to physical home searches).

Figure S9.2

Predicted probability of approving each search target across the target-matched likelihood rating, with the approval thresholds

Note. Predicted probabilities of approving each search target from the primary-sample generalized estimating equation model (E15f). Predictions average across the six conditions, weighted equally at each value on a fixed likelihood grid, with attitudes at their pooled means. Shaded regions represent two-sided 95% t confidence intervals, calculated by the delta method using the participant sandwich covariance multiplied by N/(N − p). An open circle marks the likelihood rating at which approval probability crosses .50 (E15g). A target has no threshold if its curve does not cross .50.
