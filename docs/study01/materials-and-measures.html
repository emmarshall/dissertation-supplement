Study 1 Materials and Measures

November 18, 2026
Emma Marshall, J.D., M.A.
University of Nebraska-Lincoln

The stimulus variations and study-specific survey questions are reproduced below. The stimulus materials and registered Study 1 materials and measures are also available.

Stimulus Wording

The two legal contexts differed only in the statute shown with the affidavit. Illegal abortion condition:

§ 47-101 — Unauthorized Termination of a Pregnancy; Criminal Abortion. A person commits the offense of unauthorized termination of a pregnancy when he or she (1) knowingly performs, induces, or self-induces an abortion, otherwise than as authorized by law. The offense is punishable as a Class III felony.

Improper disposal condition:

§ 47-202 — Improper Disposal of Human Skeletal Remains; Abuse of Corpse. A person commits the offense of improper disposal of human skeletal remains when he or she (1) knowingly removes, abandons, conceals, or otherwise disposes of human skeletal remains. The offense is punishable as a Class III felony.

A. Attention Checks

  1. To show you are reading each question carefully, please choose ‘Neither Agree nor Disagree.’
  2. We want to make sure your responses are thoughtful. Please choose ‘Strongly Agree’ for this item.
  3. Please read all instructions carefully. For this question, select ‘Disagree’ as your answer.

B. Probable-Cause Judgments

Foresight instructions: “Probable cause exists if all the facts and circumstances, viewed through the lens of common sense, would make a reasonably prudent person think that a search would reveal contraband or evidence of the alleged crime. Based on the description of the facts you just read, is there probable cause to justify the search?”

Hindsight instructions: “Probable cause exists if all the facts and circumstances, viewed through the lens of common sense, would make a reasonably prudent person think that a search would reveal contraband or evidence of the alleged crime. Based on the description of the facts you just read, was there probable cause to justify the search?”

  1. Probable-cause ruling. (Rachlinski et al., 2011) (Binary; No, there [IS/WAS] not probable cause to justify the search / Yes, there [IS/WAS] probable cause to justify the search)

  2. Confidence in PC ruling. “How confident are you in this decision?” (0–100 slider; 0 = not at all confident, 100 = completely confident)

  3. Subjective probability of guilt. (Baucum et al., 2018) “Based on the evidence available at the time the warrant was sought, what is the probability that the suspect committed the crime the detective suspects her of committing?” (0–100 slider; Probability (0–100%); the selected value displays with a percent sign.) Item text is Pilot Study 2’s verbatim and is identical across outcome conditions.

C. Outcome Likelihood Estimates (Fischhoff, 1975; Nestler & Egloff, 2009)

Foresight instructions: “You are the judge who has been asked to authorize this search. Based only on the affidavit you just read and what the detective knew when seeking the warrant, please rate the likelihood of each of the following outcomes of the requested search. There are many possible outcomes; please rate each outcome independently. (0 = not at all likely; 100 = completely likely)”

Hindsight instructions: “Think back to the time when the detective asked you to authorize the search, before it was carried out. Putting yourself back at that moment and deciding based only on what was known then, as if you did not know what the search ultimately revealed, please rate the likelihood of each of the following outcomes of the requested search. There are many possible outcomes; please rate each outcome independently. (0 = not at all likely; 100 = completely likely)”

  1. The search reveals evidence that the suspect illegally obtained an abortion to end her own pregnancy (e.g., records of obtaining abortion medication outside formal medical care, or related online activity). (0–100%)
  2. The search reveals evidence that the suspect improperly disposed of the fetal remains or concealed the loss following a miscarriage or stillbirth (e.g., records of disposal arrangements made after the loss, or other indications of improper handling of the remains). (0–100%)
  3. The search reveals evidence that the suspect committed an unrelated crime (e.g., stolen credit cards and associated records, or other evidence of a financial crime). (0–100%)
  4. The search reveals nothing illegal or incriminating. (0–100%)

Instructions: “Please indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with each statement as a description of the scenario you read about.”

Foresight items:

  1. The detective knows, at the time he seeks the warrant, what the search will reveal.
  2. It is difficult for the detective to predict what the search will reveal when he decides to seek the warrant. (reverse-scored)
  3. The detective has a clear and vivid idea of what the search will reveal at the time he seeks the warrant.

Hindsight items:

  1. The detective would have known all along what the search would reveal at the time he sought the warrant.
  2. It was difficult for the detective to predict what the search would reveal when he decided to seek the warrant. (reverse-scored)
  3. The detective had a clear and vivid idea of what the search would reveal at the time he sought the warrant.
  4. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, the outcome of the search was foreseeable.
  5. Overall, from the detective’s perspective, it was foreseeable that the search would uncover evidence of some offense. (general item)

Evidence Items:

  1. That an anonymous coworker reported that the suspect was no longer pregnant.

  2. That the suspect had been trying to conceive and was openly excited about her pregnancy.

  3. That the suspect had held a stable job for several years.

  4. That the suspect had told her husband, her doctor, and her workplace about her pregnancy.

  5. That the suspect had been receiving prenatal care from an obstetrician.

  6. That the suspect said she was alone when she lost her pregnancy at home.

  7. That the suspect retrieved the remains from the toilet and buried them in her backyard.

  8. That the suspect sought emergency care at the hospital after losing her pregnancy.

  9. That the suspect experienced cramping and heavy bleeding and was treated for hemorrhage and retained tissue.

  10. That the suspect was 32 years old and lived with her husband.

H. Moral Judgments of the Suspect

Suspect Mens Rea (MPC Culpability) (Kneer & Skoczeń, 2023)

Instructions: Please indicate your agreement or disagreement with each of the following statements about the suspect’s state of mind.

Strongly disagree Disagree Somewhat disagree Neither agree nor disagree Somewhat agree Agree Strongly agree
1 2 3 4 5 6 7
  1. It was the suspect’s purposeful objective to end their own pregnancy. (purposefully)
  2. The suspect was practically certain that their pregnancy would end. (knowingly)
  3. The suspect was aware of a substantial risk that their pregnancy would end. (recklessly)
  4. The suspect should have been aware of a substantial risk that their pregnancy would end. (negligently)

Moral Seriousness (Davis, 2006)

  1. Mal in se: The suspect’s conduct was morally wrong, even apart from whether the law prohibits it. (1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree)

  2. Charge Wrongness: Given the offense the suspect is suspected of committing, how morally wrong was her conduct? (1 = not at all, 4 = somewhat, 7 = very much)

Counterfactual Availability (Roese & Olson, 1996)

  1. Counterfactual: How easy is it for you to imagine an alternative to the suspect’s conduct that would have led to a different outcome? (1 = not at all easy to 7 = extremely easy)

I. Suspect’s Moral Culpability and Blame (Alicke, 2000; Malle et al., 2014)

  1. Suspect Blame: “To what extent is the suspect blameworthy for what happened?” (1 = not at all blameworthy to 7 = extremely blameworthy)
  2. Suspect Responsibility: “How responsible is the suspect for what happened?” (1 = not at all responsible to 7 = completely responsible)
  3. Suspect Punishment: “How much punishment does the suspect deserve for what happened?” (1 = no punishment at all to 7 = very severe punishment)
  4. Suspect Causality: “To what extent did the suspect’s actions cause the pregnancy loss?” (1 = not at all a cause to 7 = very strong cause)
  5. Suspect Reasons: “Did the suspect have justified reasons for her conduct during the pregnancy?” (1 = not at all justified to 7 = completely justified)
  6. Suspect Preventability: Could the suspect have prevented the pregnancy loss? (1 = definitely not to 7 = definitely yes)
  7. Suspect Character: “Given what you know about the suspect from these case materials, do you have a generally favorable or unfavorable impression of her?” (1 = very unfavorable to 7 = very favorable)
  8. Suspect Wrongness: “Was the suspect’s conduct during the pregnancy morally wrong?” (1 = not at all, 4 = somewhat, 7 = very much)

J. Detective’s Moral Culpability and Blame

  1. Officer Blame: “To what extent is the detective blameworthy for his decision to seek the warrant?” (0 = not at all blameworthy to 10 = extremely blameworthy)
  2. Officer Consequences: How severe a consequence should the detective face for his decision to seek the warrant? (0 = no consequence at all to 10 = very severe consequence)
  3. Officer Wrongness: How right or wrong was the detective’s decision to seek the warrant? (0 = not wrong at all to 10 = very wrong)

K. Search-Target Endorsement

Instructions: Imagine the same investigation you just read about. Now suppose that, instead of seeking to search the suspect’s phone, the detective stated that there was probable cause to carry out each of the searches described below. For each one, please answer the two questions that follow.

  1. Reverse keyword warrant: A court order directing a technology company to identify any person who recently searched for specified pregnancy-related or abortion-related terms on the company’s search engine.”

  2. Provider text-message content warrant: A warrant served on the suspect’s cellular-service provider compelling disclosure of the content of the suspect’s text messages.

  3. Physical home search: A warrant authorizing law enforcement officers to physically search the suspect’s home.

Participants answered two questions for each target:

  1. Search endorsement: “Would you approve of a law enforcement officer conducting this search?” (Yes / No)

  2. Certainty threshold: “What percentage certain would you have to be that the suspect committed the offense described in the affidavit before approving this search?” (Certainty required, 0–100%)

After all three targets, participants answered one further question for each target:

Instructions: “We have a few more questions about the searches you just considered. For each of the following searches, please rate how likely it is that the search would uncover evidence of the offense, assuming, hypothetically, that the suspect had committed it.”

  1. “If the suspect had indeed committed the offense described in the affidavit, what is the probability that a reverse keyword warrant on online search history would uncover evidence of it?” (0% = not at all likely; 100% = completely likely)
  2. “If the suspect had indeed committed the offense described in the affidavit, what is the probability that a warrant for the suspect’s text-message communications would uncover evidence of it?” (0% = not at all likely; 100% = completely likely)
  3. “If the suspect had indeed committed the offense described in the affidavit, what is the probability that a physical search of the suspect’s residence would uncover evidence of it?” (0% = not at all likely; 100% = completely likely)

L. Manipulation Checks

Comprehension Check

Instructions: “Please indicate which of the following statements are true about the case you just read. Check all that apply.”

  1. The suspect identified in the affidavit was named Mary Johnston. [TRUE]
  2. The suspect was married and had been actively trying to conceive. [TRUE]
  3. The suspect did not disclose the pregnancy to her workplace. [FALSE]

Outcome Manipulation Check

Instructions: “What did the search reveal at the end of the affidavit you read?”

  • Evidence consistent with the suspect’s intentional termination of the pregnancy, concealment of the loss, or improper disposal of remains [HACE]
  • Evidence of unrelated credit-card fraud [HUCE]
  • The search results were not shown; the warrant was authorized but no results were yet available [F]
  • I cannot recall what the search revealed

Legal Context Manipulation Check

Instructions: “Which of the following best describes the statute the affiant cited as the basis for seeking the warrant in the case you read?”

  • A state felony statute criminalizing the unauthorized termination of a pregnancy [Ab]
  • A state felony statute criminalizing the improper disposal of human skeletal remains [Di]
  • A federal statute governing interstate transport
  • I cannot recall which statute was cited

Gestational-Age Estimate (Exploratory)

Instructions: “Based only on what you read, about how far along do you think the pregnancy was when the loss occurred?”

  1. Early in the pregnancy (first trimester, roughly 1–12 weeks)
  2. Around the middle (early second trimester, roughly 13–20 weeks)
  3. Later in the pregnancy (late second trimester or beyond, roughly 21+ weeks)
  4. Unsure / can’t tell from the affidavit

M–S. Individual-Difference Measures and Demographics

The six individual-difference scales and the demographic items were administered with the same items in Pilot 2, Study 1, and Study 2 and are provided in the registered Study 1 materials and measures: the Modified Abortion Attitude Scale, Support for Punishment of Women for Miscarriage, the Ambivalent Sexism Inventory short form, the religiosity scale, the political-orientation items, the Outcome Preference Measure, and the demographic items.
