Notes shared by tables of this section.

  • Tables 1-S4.1 and 1-S4.11: Scaled (Satorra–Bentler) chi-square with its p; robust CFI; robust RMSEA with its 90% interval; SRMR, as registered for the over-identified model.
  • Tables 1-S4.1, 1-S4.11 and 1-S4.23: Global fit is reported and changes no model.
  • Tables 1-S4.1, 1-S4.2, 1-S4.11 and 1-S4.23: RMSEA and its 90% interval: lavaan’s robust RMSEA and its robust interval as lavaan reports them (fitMeasures() rmsea.robust, rmsea.ci.lower.robust and rmsea.ci.upper.robust, at lavaan’s default 90% level).
  • Tables 1-S4.3 and 1-S4.12: The coefficients retain the variables’ original units. I report robust (Huber–White) standard errors, two-sided normal Wald 95% intervals, and two-sided p values.
  • Tables 1-S4.3, 1-S4.12 and 1-S4.24: Paths marked equal across contexts are one shared parameter.
  • Tables 1-S4.3, 1-S4.5, 1-S4.24 and 1-S4.25: Expected evidence = evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion; unexpected evidence = financial-crime evidence; each is contrasted with no information.

  • Tables 1-S4.3, 1-S4.12 and 1-S4.24: β = the standardized estimate (std.all) of the same path; inference and decisions use the unstandardized estimates.
  • Tables 1-S4.5 and 1-S4.25: The pooled estimates: every path constrained to be equal across the two legal contexts (the registered secondary analysis when both contexts pass the legal-context rule).
  • Tables 1-S4.6 and 1-S4.15: Each entry is the condition mean with its two-sided 95% Student t interval, from the saved model’s coefficients and covariance on its residual degrees of freedom: with the attitudes, the mean at the attitude means of the participants in the model; without them, the condition mean.
  • Tables 1-S4.6 and 1-S4.8 to 1-S4.10: Likelihood ratings are in points on the 0–100 scale; foreseeability is in points on the 1–5 scale.
  • Tables 1-S4.6, 1-S4.9, 1-S4.10, 1-S4.12, 1-S4.13, 1-S4.15, 1-S4.18, 1-S4.19 and 1-S4.22: Expected evidence = evidence consistent with a self-managed abortion; unexpected evidence = financial-crime evidence.
  • Tables 1-S4.7, 1-S4.16 and 1-S4.20: Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) with the four attitudes as covariates.
  • Tables 1-S4.7, 1-S4.16 and 1-S4.20: Partial η² = partial eta-squared (η²ₚ) with its 90% CI (noncentral F).
  • Tables 1-S4.7, 1-S4.16 and 1-S4.20: Adjusted means estimated at covariate mean(s) via emmeans.
  • Tables 1-S4.7 and 1-S4.16: The bands with the attitudes are the analyses of covariance.
  • Tables 1-S4.7 and 1-S4.16: The bands without attitudes are the analyses of variance without covariates.
  • Tables 1-S4.7 and 1-S4.20: The registered tests are the comparisons, not these F tests.
  • Tables 1-S4.8 and 1-S4.17: Unstandardized slopes of the four attitudes, common to the six conditions, from the saved models with attitudes: judgment points per attitude scale point, with Student t tests and two-sided 95% Student t intervals on the residual degrees of freedom.
  • Tables 1-S4.9 and 1-S4.10: Differences of adjusted condition means with their standard errors, Student t tests and two-sided 95% Student t intervals on the residual degrees of freedom of each model; no adjustment for multiple comparisons.
  • Tables 1-S4.9 and 1-S4.18: The models carry the six conditions and no covariate.
  • Tables 1-S4.9 and 1-S4.18: Reported beside the registered models; decisions are read from the models with attitudes only.
  • Tables 1-S4.15, 1-S4.17 and 1-S4.18: Decision quality is in points on the 1–7 scale; detective blame, deserved consequences and wrongness are in points on their 0–10 scales.
  • Tables 1-S4.19 and 1-S4.22: Blame, punishment, the four mental states, responsibility and wrongness are rated on the 1–7 scale; differences are in scale points.
